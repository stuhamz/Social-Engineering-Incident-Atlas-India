## Neutral case summary

According to repeated Madhya Pradesh High Court recitals of the prosecution case, the Secretary of Ramakrishna Mission Ashram, Thatipur, Gwalior received a WhatsApp call on 17 March 2025 from a person claiming to be an Inspector from Nashik Police. The caller alleged that a money-laundering case was connected to a Canara Bank account purportedly opened in the complainant's name using Aadhaar information, and sent a PDF bank statement through WhatsApp. The caller threatened arrest, later used a video call showing a person in police uniform in a police-office-like setting, instructed the complainant not to disclose the interaction, and obtained information about the complainant and Ashram finances.

Between 17 March and 11 April 2025, the complainant transferred a total of INR 25,299,000 to accounts specified during the purported investigation. The caller promised that the funds would be returned, but communication ceased and the fraud was reported.

Subsequent High Court orders describe different evidentiary links to accused persons, including beneficiary bank accounts, withdrawals, bank facilitation, seized phones and SIMs, memorandum statements, and later detailed call-detail-record relationships. The public record reviewed supports a substantial downstream financial and communications network, while direct identification of the original WhatsApp impersonator remains incomplete.

## Reconstruction

### Target
The target was the Secretary of Ramakrishna Mission Ashram in Thatipur, Gwalior, responsible for the institution's supervision and management.

**Target-selection evidence:** not reported. The caller possessed enough personal information to present a purported bank account in the complainant's name and invoke Aadhaar-based identity misuse, but the public source does not establish how this information was obtained.

### Reconnaissance
No pre-contact reconnaissance process is directly documented. What is observable is that the caller used the complainant's identity in a specific pretext and later elicited further information about his family, activities, Ashram accounts, and funds.

### Initial contact
On 17 March 2025, the complainant received a WhatsApp call from a person claiming to be an Inspector from Nashik Police.

### Pretext
The caller claimed that a money-laundering FIR had been registered, that a Canara Bank account had been opened in the complainant's name using Aadhaar details, and that approximately INR 20 crore had moved through the account. A PDF account statement bearing the complainant's name was sent by WhatsApp.

### Social-engineering mechanisms

- **Authority:** police identity and purported investigative authority.
- **Fear:** threat of arrest and penal consequences.
- **Urgency:** warning that police could arrive if cooperation was refused.
- **Trust / legitimacy:** purported statement, police uniform, and police-office-like setting.
- **Isolation:** instructions not to move without permission or disclose the matter to seniors/family.
- **Repeated contact:** continuing WhatsApp voice/video calls.
- **Information elicitation:** personal, family, activity, Ashram-account, and fund information.

### Requested action
Cooperate with the purported investigation, remain isolated from outside verification, disclose information, and transfer Ashram funds to specified accounts.

### Victim action
The complainant continued communicating and transferred INR 25,299,000 between 17 March and 11 April 2025.

### Consequence
The Ashram lost INR 25,299,000 according to repeated court recitals. An early NDTV report states the transfers went to 12 bank accounts.

## Attribution analysis

### Attribution target
Two levels are separated:

1. the original social-engineering operator(s) who conducted the Nashik Police impersonation
2. the downstream network allegedly connected to accounts, withdrawals, bank facilitation, devices, or communications

### Primary basis
The strongest public downstream evidence is a combination of bank/money-flow records, cash-out activity, CDRs, device/SIM association, and accused-specific transaction links.

### Alternative explanation
A person connected to an account, withdrawal, SIM, device, or another accused may have played a narrower role than the original caller. Knowledge of the broader scheme is contested for several accused and remains trial-dependent.

### Attribution strength
**Moderate at incident level.**

There is meaningful multi-source evidence linking identified people to parts of the downstream network. The original impersonating caller is not conclusively identified in the reviewed public material.

### Limitations

- no reviewed source provides authenticated WhatsApp provider records identifying the original operator
- no reviewed source provides a complete forensic extraction tying the impersonation account to a specific accused
- bank-account ownership or receipt of funds is not equivalent to caller identity
- CDR linkage shows communication patterns, not content or intent
- several assertions remain prosecution allegations or prima facie bail-stage assessments

## Primary evidentiary gap
Direct attribution of the original WhatsApp impersonation account through provider records, authenticated account metadata, device artefacts, telecom correlation, and a clear chain tying the account to a human operator.
