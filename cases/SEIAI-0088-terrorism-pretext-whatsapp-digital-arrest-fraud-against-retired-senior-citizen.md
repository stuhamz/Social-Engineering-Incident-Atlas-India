## Procedural posture

A. Javith, described as A1, had been arrested on 20 May 2026. The Madras High Court, Madurai Bench dismissed his bail petition on 6 July 2026. The investigation remained pending and A2, described in the order as the main accused, was still absconding.

## Neutral case summary

A retired senior citizen in Madurai was allegedly contacted on WhatsApp and told that illegal transactions linked to terrorist organisations had passed through his account and that he had been digitally arrested. He was induced to transfer INR 8.5 million through online banking on multiple occasions. In denying bail to A1 A. Javith, the High Court recorded the prosecution case and a prima facie view that he had participated in the threatening digital-arrest interaction. The source does not reproduce independent platform, telecom, device or beneficiary-account attribution, so the role coding remains conservative.

## Reconstruction

### Initial contact and pretext
According to the prosecution allegation recited in the order, A1, with the help of A2, made a WhatsApp call to the complainant. The complainant was told that illegal transactions connected with terrorist organisations had been routed through his account and that he was digitally arrested.

### Requested action and outcome
Money was demanded through online banking. The complainant transferred **INR 8,500,000** on various occasions before becoming suspicious and reporting the matter to police.

## Evidence and disputed claims

The Court states that it reviewed the FIR and connected materials. The defence asserted that INR 21 lakh had been frozen, while the State expressly disputed that claim and said no amount had been frozen before the money was withdrawn by other accused. The freeze claim is therefore not coded as an established fact.
