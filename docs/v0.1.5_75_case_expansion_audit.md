# v0.1.5 75-Case Corrective Expansion Audit

## Scope

v0.1.5 expands the Atlas from **50 to 75 reviewed incident records** and from **148 to 198 reviewed actor-role records** without changing the v0.1.2 actor-role schema.

Cases 51–75 were selected in response to the v0.1.4 analytical-stability audit. The batch was not designed to estimate prevalence. It was designed to reduce source-stage concentration, widen temporal coverage, add underrepresented mechanisms, and bring more final/appellate adjudicatory material into the corpus.

## Final counts

- Incident records: **75**
- Actor-role records: **198**
- Registered sources: **83**
- Screening candidates: **96**
  - included: **75**
  - pending: **9**
  - excluded: **9**
  - duplicates: **3**
- States/UT labels represented: **21**
- Incident validator: **0 errors, 0 warnings**
- Actor validator: **0 errors, 0 warnings**

## Corrective batch source-stage composition

Cases 51–75 contain:

- `final_judgment`: **10**
- `appellate_judgment`: **10**
- `bail_order`: **4**
- `interim_order`: **1**

This materially changes the full-corpus source-stage profile.

### Full-corpus source-stage distribution

- `bail_order`: **37/75 (49.3%)**
- `appellate_judgment`: **19/75 (25.3%)**
- `final_judgment`: **14/75 (18.7%)**
- `interim_order`: **4/75 (5.3%)**
- `procedural_order`: **1/75 (1.3%)**

At v0.1.4, bail orders were 33/50 (66%) and final/appellate judgments together were 13/50 (26%). At v0.1.5, bail orders fall below half of the corpus and final/appellate judgments together rise to **33/75 (44%)**.

This is the strongest methodological improvement in the expansion.

## Category and source-stage diversity

The corpus now contains **18 primary attack categories**, including the first primary `vishing` record.

The number of primary categories occurring in only one source stage falls from **10 of 17** at v0.1.4 to **5 of 18** at v0.1.5.

The five still confined to one primary source stage are:

- `family_impersonation` → bail orders
- `tech_support_fraud` → bail orders
- `marketplace_fraud` → appellate judgments
- `smishing` → appellate judgments
- `vishing` → appellate judgments

Category comparisons therefore remain source-stage sensitive, but the confounding is materially lower than at 50 cases.

## Temporal coverage

The corrective batch includes incident years from **2011 through 2025**. Eighteen of the 25 new incidents pre-date 2024, and only two are coded to 2025.

As a result, 2025 incidents rise only from 21 to 23 while the corpus grows from 50 to 75. Their corpus share falls from **42.0% to 30.7%**.

This improves temporal balance, but the dataset remains retrieval-driven rather than time-representative.

## Geography

The corpus now represents **21 state/UT labels**, up from 20.

Delhi and Haryana increase from 23 cases to 32 cases, but because the total corpus grows faster their combined share falls from **46.0% to 42.7%**.

This is only a modest geographic correction. Delhi remains the largest single source geography with **21/75 cases**, so geography remains an explicit limitation for future collection.

## Source corroboration

This expansion did **not** solve the single-source problem.

- v0.1.4: **47/50** cases had exactly one registered source
- v0.1.5: **71/75** cases have exactly one registered source

One additional contemporaneous T1 order was registered for SEIAI-0066, but weak or duplicative secondary sources were not added merely to improve a metric.

The appropriate interpretation is therefore:

> Source-stage quality improved substantially; independent source depth did not.

Second-source retrieval should remain a priority for future work, especially where the primary material is bail-stage or where attribution turns on disputed investigative claims.

## Actor-layer stability

The actor schema again handled the expansion without structural change.

Full actor-role distribution:

- `strong`: **7**
- `moderate`: **59**
- `limited`: **34**
- `unclear`: **82**
- `not_assessed`: **16**

There are now **47 cases** containing both a victim-facing and a financial actor layer. Using the same conservative three-level comparison as the v0.1.4 audit (`moderate/strong` > `limited` > `unclear/not_assessed`):

- financial-layer attribution is stronger in **36/47**
- the layers are equal in **11/47**
- victim-facing attribution is stronger in **0/47**

At row level:

- **61/70** victim-facing actor records are `unclear` or `not_assessed`
- **58/69** financial actor records have at least `limited` attribution
- **33/69** financial actor records are `moderate` or `strong`

The pattern therefore survives a batch deliberately enriched with non-bail adjudicatory material. It remains a descriptive property of this purposive corpus, not a national-frequency estimate.

As before, attribution basis and attribution strength must not be treated as statistically independent variables because evidentiary quality is partly built into the strength rubric.

## Batch-level methodological decisions

1. **No schema redesign.** The existing incident and actor tables represented all 25 additions.
2. **SIM-swap cases were deliberately capped.** They are useful because final/appellate telecom decisions provide unusually strong evidence of authentication failure, but they were not allowed to consume the entire corrective batch.
3. **Focal-incident rule preserved.** A network-level call-centre case was excluded when the public source did not isolate a sufficiently clean focal victim incident.
4. **Duplicate handling preserved.** Iqbal Rashid was rediscovered during appellate targeting and logged as a duplicate of SEIAI-0037 rather than recoded.
5. **No forced corroboration.** A thin secondary article was not treated as methodologically valuable merely because the corpus has a single-source limitation.
6. **Consumer/telecom adjudications are not offender convictions.** They can establish or discuss phishing/SIM/authentication mechanisms without establishing criminal identity.
7. **No prevalence claims.** Counts remain properties of a purposive research corpus.

## Validation and privacy

Both structural validators return **0 errors and 0 warnings**.

The 25 newly generated researcher-authored case notes were scanned for email addresses, Indian mobile-number patterns and 12–18 digit identifier strings. No such raw identifiers were detected.

## Bottom line

v0.1.5 is a stronger research corpus than v0.1.4 primarily because it changes **what kinds of legal records the Atlas is built from**, not simply because it adds 25 rows.

The next expansion should not immediately optimize for 100 cases. Before another large batch, the most valuable work is likely to be:

1. targeted second-source corroboration;
2. geographic expansion outside Delhi/Haryana;
3. final/appellate material for `family_impersonation` and `tech_support_fraud`;
4. deeper exploitation of the actor-layer attribution result; and
5. a decision on `accused_count` before the next breaking schema version.
