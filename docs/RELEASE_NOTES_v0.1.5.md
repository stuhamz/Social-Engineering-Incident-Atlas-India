# Release Notes v0.1.5

## 75-case corrective expansion

v0.1.5 expands the Social Engineering Incident Atlas India from **50 to 75 reviewed incidents** and from **148 to 198 reviewed actor-role records**.

The expansion was directed by the v0.1.4 analytical-stability audit rather than by a simple case-count target.

### Included in this release

- 75 reviewed incident records
- 198 reviewed actor-role attribution records
- 83 registered public sources
- 96 logged screening candidates
- 25 new narrative case reconstructions
- 10 new final-judgment primary sources
- 10 new appellate-judgment primary sources
- first primary `vishing` case
- stronger coverage of phishing, SIM-swap/account takeover, smishing, customer-support manipulation, job/government impersonation and social-media/deepfake investment impersonation
- updated 75-case expansion audit and metrics
- validation tooling with zero incident and actor errors/warnings

### Main methodological effect

Primary bail-order dependence falls from **33/50 (66%)** to **37/75 (49.3%)**.

Final and appellate judgments together rise from **13/50 (26%)** to **33/75 (44%)**.

Categories confined to a single source stage fall from **10/17** to **5/18**.

### Important limitation

Independent source depth remains weak: **71/75 incidents have exactly one registered source**. The release therefore improves source-stage quality much more than source corroboration.

### Actor-layer result

Using the same conservative comparison as the 50-case audit, 47 incidents now contain both victim-facing and financial layers:

- financial stronger: 36
- equal: 11
- victim-facing stronger: 0

This remains descriptive of the corpus and must not be interpreted as a national cybercrime statistic.

Research principle:

> **Reconstruct broadly. Attribute conservatively.**
