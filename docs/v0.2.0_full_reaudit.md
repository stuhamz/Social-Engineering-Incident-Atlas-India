# v0.2.0 Full Corpus Re-Audit

## Scope

This release rechecks the complete Atlas history from `SEIAI-0001` through `SEIAI-0075` before any further paper development or horizontal expansion.

The audit covers:

- every incident row;
- every active actor-role row;
- every registered source relationship;
- screening and duplicate history;
- incident-date precision;
- source stage and source type;
- actor identity resolution;
- legacy role-layer coding;
- non-exclusive victim-facing and financial functions;
- case/source/actor referential integrity;
- public narrative consistency;
- and repository validation.

The governing principle remains:

> **Reconstruct broadly. Attribute conservatively.**

## Duplicate retirement

`SEIAI-0060` and `SEIAI-0029` encoded the same *State Bank of India v. Rajesh Garg* OLX/UPI incident, using the same primary-source URL and the same INR 64,000 loss.

v0.2.0:

- retains `SEIAI-0029`, the earlier stable ID;
- retires `SEIAI-0060`;
- retires `SRC-SEIAI-0060-01` from the active source registry;
- changes `CAND-0079` to `duplicate` of `SEIAI-0029`;
- and does **not** renumber `SEIAI-0061` through `SEIAI-0075`.

## Actor-model repair

The old `role_layer` field remains useful as descriptive taxonomy, but it is not a mutually exclusive analytical outcome.

v0.2.0 adds:

- `victim_facing_function`
- `financial_function`
- `function_assignment_basis`

These are coded independently. An actor can perform both functions.

The full 196-row active actor table now has:

- victim-facing yes / financial no: **74**
- victim-facing no / financial yes: **79**
- both yes: **8**
- neither yes: **31**
- financial uncertain: **3**
- both uncertain: **1**

These are coding distributions, not prevalence estimates.

## Human identity-resolution corrections

Four actor rows required definite identity-resolution corrections under the tightened rule:

- `SEIAI-0019-A01`: `unknown` to `partially_identified`
- `SEIAI-0071-A01`: `unknown` to `partially_identified`
- `SEIAI-0048-A01`: `partially_identified` to `unknown`
- `SEIAI-0065-A01`: `partially_identified` to `unknown`

The rule is explicit: persona names, aliases, numbered accused, bank/SIM/device/IP endpoints and generic conspiracy language do not by themselves resolve a specific real-world human.

## Date-precision repair

The audit removed or corrected exact dates where the old Atlas had encoded detection, complaint, FIR or judgment timing as though it were attack conduct, or had created unsupported year-boundary dates.

Notable corrections include `SEIAI-0055`, `SEIAI-0057`, `SEIAI-0058`, `SEIAI-0062`, `SEIAI-0065`, `SEIAI-0066`, `SEIAI-0067`, `SEIAI-0068`, `SEIAI-0071`, `SEIAI-0072`, `SEIAI-0073` and `SEIAI-0075`.

Where only a broad year or period is defensible, exact day-level fields are now blank. `incident_year` can be `not_reported` rather than inferred from a procedural date.

## 51-75 batch harmonization

The v0.1.5 corrective batch had passed structural validators but was not populated to the same descriptive completeness standard as earlier records.

v0.2.0 backfills source-supported:

- case numbers;
- target sectors;
- platform details;
- impersonated-identity detail;
- and secondary attack categories where the source supports them.

Unsupported optional fields remain blank rather than being filled for cosmetic completeness.

## Audit outcomes

Across the 75-ID history:

- **40** IDs required no substantive correction after source recheck;
- **34** active IDs received one or more substantive incident, actor or source corrections;
- **1** ID was retired as a duplicate.

A correction does not necessarily mean the incident was wrong. Many corrections are harmonization, date-precision, identity-resolution or schema-function repairs.

The complete row-by-row history is in `docs/v0.2.0_case_audit_log.csv`.

## Validation

v0.2.0 runs three validators:

1. `validate_dataset.py`
2. `validate_actors.py`
3. `validate_full_audit.py`

The third validator adds checks absent from earlier releases, including:

- semantic duplicate detection through active cross-case source URLs;
- retired-ID leakage;
- source-to-case ownership;
- actor-to-source ownership;
- date ordering and year consistency;
- normalized case-number propagation;
- and actor functional coding.

All three validators return **0 errors and 0 warnings**.

## Remaining limitations

The audit improves internal consistency. It does not turn this into a representative national sample.

Current active corpus limitations include:

- 37/74 primary sources are bail orders;
- 70/74 incidents have only one registered source;
- 32/74 cases are from Delhi or Haryana;
- 23/74 incidents are coded to 2025;
- public judicial records are selected documents whose purpose affects which actors and evidence become visible;
- provider-side logs, forensic provenance and human identity behind digital endpoints often remain absent.

The corrected dataset should therefore be evaluated for research quality **before** another expansion or a results-driven manuscript is pursued.
