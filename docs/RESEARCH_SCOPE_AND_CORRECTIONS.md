# Research scope, sources and post-release corrections

## Purpose

The Atlas is a *purposive public-source research corpus*, not a national crime-statistics series or a list of people proven guilty of offences. It records how social-engineering incidents are described in available documentary sources and how the evidence is connected to distinct actor functions.

An analytically important distinction is between a person who communicated directly with a target and a person whom records connect to financial accounts, a phone, a device or some other evidential endpoint. The latter link does not alone prove who initiated a deceptive interaction.

## Relationship to narrower academic studies

The 300-case Atlas can identify relevant judicial materials for a focused analysis of courts' evidentiary reasoning. Such a study should independently select and read its judgments, record the exact accused-and-charge propositions, distinguish acquittals from convictions on other charges, and separately assess the source text and its provenance. The Atlas does not itself establish the conclusions of that narrower study.

## Sources and limitations

The frozen corpus contains 300 incident records, 600 actor-role coding rows, 310 registered source entries and 412 screening decisions. It is not a random or representative sample. Many records come from bail or interim proceedings, and some are hosted on legal-publication mirrors. A source being coded T1 does not by itself prove an issuer-hosted original was independently authenticated. One source may record multiple evidential traces without those traces constituting independent corroborating sources.

“Reviewed” refers to the project's first-reader review and structural consistency checks; it must not be read as external academic certification. An entry's actor-attribution category is an analytical code, not a criminal conviction.

## Corrections after the v0.4.0 archive

The 2 November 2026 working-tree update retains the frozen corpus population while applying 10 structured field edits across four cases, 44 revised lines across 16 case narratives and 24 filename neutralizations. Several changes remove unnecessary identifying context. The original Zenodo v0.4.0 archival dataset and earlier Git commit are not silently replaced.

In SEIAI-0281 the summary outcome was too broad: the source reports a mixed disposition, with convictions for some offences and acquittal of the online-impersonation charge. The corrected structured field and narrative preserve that distinction. Source-origin verification remains a separate question. SEIAI-0259 remains qualified because a companion Section 174A order was not separately authenticated in the underlying review.

Some other cases require original court documents or a second independent reading before more specific assertions can be made. Individual entries should be read with their procedural status and source limitations in view.

**Citing the work:** Cite the Zenodo DOI only when relying on the original v0.4.0 snapshot. Cite the current repository commit for post-release corrections. Any subsequent paper's sampled judgments require their own direct citations.
