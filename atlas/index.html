ABOUT THIS CORPUS

A research index, not a national crime tally

These 300 incidents were selected from accessible sources. Counts below describe what this dataset contains, not how often scams happen in India. Most cases rely on one registered primary source, and many sources are bail orders rather than final trial findings. A documented trace is not proof of the accused person's role.

DOCUMENTARY EVIDENCE EXPLORER

What kinds of evidence do the records mention?

A report of an account, phone, IP address or device can establish an evidentiary endpoint without identifying the person responsible for an interaction. Use the controls to compare how records discuss different types of traces.

How to interpret the coding

Yes: evidence type is reported in the record. No: explicitly coded no. Not reported: available material does not establish whether it existed. Not applicable: the field is not applicable. These are researcher codes, not authenticated forensic findings.

Evidence-status distribution

Evidence links and attribution

Financial account ownership, IP association and possession of a device are distinct propositions from the identity of a caller or the accused person's knowledge and intent. A criminal court may accept one link but reject another.

Review the attribution framework.

Electronic record questions

Admissibility and authentication of electronic records are separate from what the admitted material proves. A code indicating that authentication was discussed is not a finding that authentication succeeded.

SOURCE AND PROVENANCE EXPLORER

What documents is this dataset built from?

The Atlas registers 310 sources for 300 incidents. The primary-source stage distributions below use the case-level field; source-tier charts use the 310-row registry, so their denominators differ.

Registered source tiers (310 records)

T1: adjudicatory material; T2: institutional; T3: journalism.

Primary document stage (300 cases)

Bail and interim material often recount allegations, not guilt findings.

What can source classification establish?

A judgment reproduced on a legal information website is not automatically an authenticated issuer-hosted original. A judicial source can describe allegations, arguments, interim observations or final findings, depending on procedural posture. These must remain distinguishable.

Open the source registry to inspect source IDs and provenance, and consult original documents wherever available. The website does not reproduce names, raw documents or source URLs in its browser data payload.

RESEARCH METHODS AND LIMITS

How to read this Atlas responsibly

1. What is the dataset?

A purposively collected corpus of 300 publicly documented incidents in which social engineering is materially relevant. It is not a representative survey or national cybercrime registry.

2. What do the charts count?

Records and coding fields in this corpus, not all incidents in India. A single case can count towards multiple evidence and mechanism charts, and a registered source may cover more than one observation.

3. What does “reviewed” mean?

First-reader project coding and structural checks. It does not mean independent judicial-source authentication, peer review, a second-reader check of every case, or a legal determination of guilt.

4. What is attribution?

The documented connection from an act or evidentiary endpoint to a particular person and conduct. A bank account receiving money may be established without identifying the original impersonator.

5. What about the publication history?

The Zenodo v0.4.0 DOI is an immutable historical snapshot. The GitHub repository contains documented later corrections; cite the exact repository revision when using those corrections.

6. How can the material be reused?

Read individual case notes alongside registered primary sources. For journal work, independently verify the selected judgments and analyze the accused and charge, not an undifferentiated incident-level guilt label.
