# Source Quality Framework

Source tier and source stage are recorded separately.

## Tier T1: Judicial material

Examples:

- final judgments
- appellate judgments
- bail orders
- interim or procedural orders

Important: a bail or interim order may accurately report that an allegation was made without establishing the truth of the allegation. Preserve the procedural stage.

## Tier T2: Official institutional material

Examples:

- police releases
- government releases
- regulator notices
- investigative-agency material
- official FIR or charge-sheet summaries where publicly available

These may describe an investigation position rather than an adjudicated fact.

## Tier T3: Credible journalism

Use reports that contain substantive incident detail and identify their sources where possible.

Journalistic reports may supplement stronger sources or support cases where primary material is unavailable, but claims must be attributed to the report.

## Claim discipline

For every material statement, distinguish among:

- **judicial finding**
- **prosecution or investigative allegation**
- **defence claim**
- **victim or witness account**
- **journalistic report**
- **researcher inference**

Do not silently convert one category into another.

## Access host versus source type

The tier classifies the **underlying material**, not the website that hosts the accessible copy.

For example, a High Court order accessed through a legal-search mirror is coded as judicial material, while the exact access URL is recorded in `references/sources.csv`. The repository should not describe a mirror URL as an official court-hosted URL.
